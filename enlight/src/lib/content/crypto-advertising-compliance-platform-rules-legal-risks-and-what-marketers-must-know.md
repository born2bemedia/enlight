A crypto advertising campaign can be rejected even when its claims are accurate. It can also be approved by an advertising platform and still breach local financial promotion rules. These are separate risks, and treating them as one problem can cost a project its advertising budget, its accounts and, in serious cases, its ability to operate in a target market.

Crypto marketing requires more than persuasive copy and efficient media buying. Exchanges, wallets, token issuers, trading tools, investment services and blockchain businesses can face different advertising restrictions depending on their products and target audiences. The rules also vary between Google, Meta, TikTok, X and Microsoft Advertising.

For crypto projects, compliance must be considered before a campaign is planned—not after an ad is rejected. That means understanding platform eligibility, local financial regulations, permitted claims, landing-page requirements and the responsibilities of influencers and affiliate partners.

## 1. Google Ads: Certification, Product Restrictions and Eligible Markets

Google Ads has a dedicated **[Cryptocurrencies and related products policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14009787?hl=en)**. It distinguishes between products that are prohibited, products that may be advertised with certification and categories that do not require crypto-specific certification.

**Certification does not make every crypto product eligible.** Google's policy prohibits advertising certain categories regardless of location, including ICO presales and public offerings, DeFi trading protocols, cryptocurrency loans, initial DEX offerings, token liquidity pools, unhosted software wallets and unregulated decentralised applications. Cryptocurrency trading signals, investment advice and affiliate or comparison destinations containing related content can also fall within prohibited categories.

Other products may be advertised only under specific conditions. In the European Union, for example, Google permits ads for cryptocurrency exchanges and software wallets in a defined set of countries when the advertiser is authorised as a Crypto-Asset Service Provider (CASP) under MiCA by the relevant national authority and has Google's required certification. Other product categories and countries have their own conditions.

The distinction matters because a business can be legally registered without meeting Google's advertising requirements. Equally, certification from Google does not replace the legal authorisation required in a market.

Before building a campaign, marketers should verify:

- Whether the exact product category is permitted.
- Whether the target country is on Google's approved list for that category.
- Whether the advertiser needs regulatory authorisation and Google certification.
- Whether the ad, website and destination content comply with other Google policies.

Google states that cryptocurrency policy requirements are updated as the market evolves. Its current guidance also indicates that applications for crypto advertising certification move into the Google Ads account workflow from June 2026. Teams should check the live policy and application process before launch rather than rely on old campaign documentation.

## 2. Meta Ads: Product Eligibility, Permission and Landing-Page Consistency

Facebook and Instagram offer access to large audiences, but crypto advertisers should not assume that a standard Meta Ads account is sufficient to promote any financial product.

Meta applies restrictions to cryptocurrency-related advertising, with requirements depending on the product and the applicable policy. Certain crypto products and services require prior written permission from Meta. Other blockchain-related content, such as educational or informational material, may be treated differently from ads that directly promote a financial product.

The starting point is Meta's **[Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/)**. Advertisers should check the current requirements for their precise product rather than interpret permission for one category as approval for all crypto advertising.

For example, an educational campaign about blockchain technology is not necessarily equivalent to an ad encouraging users to open an exchange account, buy a token or use a crypto investment service. A wallet, token offering, trading service and blockchain software product may trigger different reviews.

The campaign must also be evaluated as a complete user experience. Review the ad creative, headline, call to action, destination URL and landing page together. If an ad describes a service as a simple educational tool but the landing page promotes speculative trading, the discrepancy can create policy problems. The same applies when the ad omits material limitations or makes claims that the destination cannot substantiate.

Before launch, confirm the current permission requirements, any relevant regulatory or geographic restrictions, and the consistency of the ad and landing page. Keep records of approvals and the materials submitted for review. A previous approval should not be treated as permanent permission for a changed product, claim or target market.

## 3. TikTok Ads: Country-Specific Rules and Age Restrictions

TikTok's **[Financial Services policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-financial-services)** makes clear that financial advertising requirements depend on the delivery market. In general, advertisers must comply with local law, hold relevant licences where required, include applicable disclosures and restrict financial-service ads to audiences aged 18 or older. The detailed rules can be stricter for specific products and countries.

Some markets allow cryptocurrency exchange or custodial wallet advertising only when the advertiser is properly licensed, obtains permission through TikTok's application process and applies the required age restrictions. Other markets prohibit particular crypto products entirely. Initial coin offerings and misleading get-rich-quick offers are among the categories that can be prohibited.

This makes geographic targeting a compliance issue, not simply a media-buying decision. A campaign that is permitted in one country may be restricted or prohibited in another, even if the creative and product remain unchanged.

Marketers should also avoid assuming that a broad financial-services approval covers every crypto product. TikTok's policy distinguishes among exchanges, custodial wallets, non-custodial wallets, tokens, NFTs and other services. Its requirements can differ by category.

Before buying traffic, check the market-specific section of the current policy and confirm the approval route with TikTok where necessary. Do not launch first and rely on the platform to explain the restrictions after rejection.

## 4. X Ads: Crypto Promotion Depends on Product and Jurisdiction

X permits some advertising for financial products, cryptocurrencies and NFTs, but applies restrictions based on the product and the country being targeted. Its **[Financial Services policy](https://help.x.com/en/business-and-advertising/financial-services)** identifies categories that may be permitted only after the advertiser meets country-specific licensing requirements and obtains the required certification.

Crypto exchanges and wallets, for example, are subject to market-specific restrictions. Some categories, including ICOs and certain mining-related promotions, are prohibited. Approval for one category does not automatically extend to another: X explicitly states that advertisers must submit separate certification requests for different covered categories, such as NFTs and cryptocurrency products.

This distinction is particularly relevant to projects promoting several services under one brand. A company might advertise educational content, a wallet and an exchange, but the eligibility of one does not establish the eligibility of the others.

The platform's rules should therefore be checked at the campaign level. The product being promoted, the destination website and the target jurisdiction all matter. Teams should also review their ads whenever the service, geographic targeting or claims change.

## 5. Microsoft Advertising: Prior Approval and Business Eligibility

Microsoft Advertising also restricts cryptocurrency promotion. Its **[Cryptocurrency Advertising Program](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/forms/policies/cryptocurrency-advertising-application)** states that only approved advertisers can advertise crypto-related content and that submitting an application does not guarantee approval.

The application process asks for business and domain information, target markets and a link to the relevant risk disclaimer. Its published eligibility conditions include requirements for exchanges, such as a minimum operating history of 18 months. The permitted markets are limited to those listed in the application process.

Microsoft also operates a separate **[Cryptocurrency Wallet Pilot Program](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/forms/policies/cryptocurrency-wallet-pilot-program)** for approved wallet advertisers. Its conditions differ from those for exchanges, including market availability and wallet eligibility.

For a marketing team, the practical implication is straightforward: do not build a forecast that assumes Microsoft traffic will be available until the advertiser's product, market and account eligibility have been confirmed. An application is not the same as approval, and approval for an exchange should not be assumed to cover a wallet or another product.

## 6. Reddit Ads: Policy Review Still Applies to Financial and Crypto Products

Reddit can be relevant to crypto projects because users discuss specific tokens, protocols, wallets and industry developments in topic-focused communities. However, an audience's interest in crypto does not automatically make an ad eligible.

Reddit's **[Advertising Policy Overview](https://business.reddithelp.com/articles/Knowledge/Reddit-Advertising-Policy-Overview)** identifies financial and cryptocurrency products and services as a dedicated policy category. Ads are reviewed for compliance, and repeated violations can result in restrictions or account suspension. Reddit also reserves the right to reject or remove ads at its discretion.

Advertisers should check the specific financial and cryptocurrency policy before launch, including any restrictions that apply to the product and target market. They should not assume that an ad permitted on another social network will pass Reddit's review.

Community fit matters as well. Even a compliant campaign may perform poorly if it looks like generic promotion in a community that expects technical evidence, transparent disclosures and substantive discussion. Compliance establishes whether an ad may run; it does not guarantee that the message will be credible or effective.

## 7. Legal Requirements: Platform Approval Is Not Legal Approval

Advertising platforms enforce their own rules, but crypto businesses must also consider the laws that apply to their products and audiences. These obligations may apply to websites, social posts, influencer content, paid ads and other communications—not only to advertisements purchased through a platform.

**European Union: MiCA and Marketing Communications**<br>
The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) includes requirements for certain crypto-asset marketing communications. Under **[Article 7](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-7-marketing-communications)**, marketing communications relating to offers to the public or admission to trading of crypto-assets covered by that article must be clearly identifiable as marketing, fair, clear and not misleading, and consistent with the relevant crypto-asset white paper where one is required. The article also specifies information and statements that must be included.

Separate provisions apply to asset-referenced tokens, e-money tokens and crypto-asset service providers. For example, **[Article 66](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-66-obligation-act-honestly-fairly)** requires crypto-asset service providers to communicate fairly, clearly and without misleading clients, including in marketing communications, and to warn clients about crypto-asset risks.

These requirements should not be treated as a universal checklist for every blockchain-related business. Applicability depends on the product, activity and relevant MiCA provisions. A campaign promoting a software tool may raise different issues from a public token offer or a regulated crypto-asset service.

**United Kingdom: Financial Promotions for Cryptoassets**<br>
The UK's financial promotions regime applies to qualifying cryptoasset promotions targeting UK consumers, including communications by firms based overseas. The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) explains that the rules cover websites, mobile apps, social media and online advertising.

Under the regime, promotions must use a lawful communication route. Applicable rules can require specific risk warnings, restrictions on incentives to invest, a 24-hour cooling-off period and other consumer-protection measures. The exact requirements depend on the promotion and the firm's role.

The consequences of getting this wrong can be serious. The FCA states that communicating qualifying cryptoasset promotions to UK consumers outside the permitted routes can breach section 21 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, a criminal offence punishable by up to two years' imprisonment, an unlimited fine, or both.

See the FCA's **[guidance for cryptoasset firms marketing to UK consumers](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/cryptoassets/marketing-uk-consumers)** and its **[finalised guidance on cryptoasset financial promotions](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/fg23-3-finalised-non-handbook-guidance-cryptoasset-financial-promotions)**.

**United States: Truthful Claims and Influencer Disclosures**<br>
In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's advertising principles require claims to be truthful, non-deceptive and supported by appropriate evidence. Crypto campaigns can create additional concerns when they promise profits, misrepresent risk or use testimonials that give consumers an inaccurate impression of likely results.

The FTC's **[endorsement and influencer guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews)** explains that material relationships between brands and endorsers must be disclosed clearly. Payment is not the only relevant relationship: free products, perks and other benefits can also require disclosure. A disclosure tool provided by a platform does not necessarily resolve every legal obligation.

The legal analysis depends on the product, the claim and the audience. Crypto firms promoting investments or financial services may also need to consider other applicable federal and state requirements. A disclaimer alone does not make a misleading claim acceptable.

## 8. What Non-Compliant Crypto Advertising Can Cost a Project

A rejected ad is the most visible outcome, but it is not the only one. The consequences can extend from wasted campaign spend to regulatory action.

| Risk | How it affects the project |
| --- | --- |
| Ad rejection | Creative work and campaign preparation are delayed; launch plans may need to change |
| Account restrictions or suspension | Paid acquisition can stop, and access to an established advertising account may be affected |
| Wasted budget | Research, production, agency fees and media planning may deliver no usable campaign |
| Regulatory action | Depending on the law and breach, a project may face enforcement, financial penalties, orders to remove promotions or criminal consequences |
| Reputational damage | Users, partners and investors may question the project's claims, governance or credibility |
| Poor-quality acquisition | Misleading messaging can attract unsuitable users, increase complaints and undermine long-term retention |

The severity depends on the facts. A platform-policy breach is not automatically a legal violation, and a legal violation does not always result in the same penalty. But teams should not treat repeated rejections as a minor operational inconvenience: they can signal a deeper mismatch between the product, the claims and the rules governing the market.

## 9. Why General Marketing Teams Can Miss Crypto Compliance Risks

A general marketing team may be highly competent at paid acquisition, creative testing and conversion optimisation while lacking the product and regulatory knowledge needed for crypto campaigns.

The problem often begins with categorisation. A marketer may see an exchange as another fintech product, a token as a digital product launch or a wallet as a standard software application. Advertising policies and financial promotion rules may treat those categories very differently.

The next risk is messaging. Claims such as “guaranteed returns,” “risk-free trading,” “earn passive income” or “the next 100x token” can create serious problems when they imply outcomes that cannot be substantiated or minimise material risk. Even less obvious claims can mislead if they use selective performance data, omit important conditions or imply regulatory approval that the business does not hold.

A third risk is the gap between the ad and the product. The creative may be compliant in isolation, but the landing page may introduce a prohibited offer, make stronger claims or target users who cannot legally access the service. Compliance therefore needs to cover the full conversion path, not just the headline and banner.

Crypto-focused teams are better positioned to recognise these issues because they understand how products are structured, how crypto audiences evaluate claims, and how platform restrictions interact with market-specific requirements. This does not replace legal advice. It helps identify the right questions early and prevents avoidable mistakes from reaching production.

## 10. A Practical Compliance Check Before Launch

Every campaign should have a documented review before money is committed. At minimum, the team should confirm the following:

- **Product eligibility:** Is the exact product permitted on the chosen platform?
- **Market eligibility:** Is advertising allowed in each target country, and are licences or certifications required?
- **Claims and evidence:** Can every material claim be substantiated? Are returns, risks and limitations presented accurately?
- **Creative and landing page:** Do the ad, destination page, disclosures and user journey communicate the same offer?
- **Targeting:** Are age, location and user-eligibility restrictions correctly configured?
- **Partners and tracking:** Are influencers, affiliates, disclosures, data collection and measurement practices compliant with applicable requirements?
- **Ongoing monitoring:** Who checks policy changes, manages approvals and reviews campaigns when the product, claim or target market changes?

The review should be repeated when the campaign changes—not only when a new campaign is created. A new target country, revised landing page, new token feature or different influencer arrangement can change the compliance assessment.

## Conclusion: Compliance Is Part of Acquisition Strategy

Crypto advertising is not governed by one universal set of rules. Google, Meta, TikTok, X, Microsoft Advertising and Reddit apply different eligibility criteria, while national and regional laws can impose additional requirements on the same campaign.

The safest approach is to identify the product and target market first, verify the platform's current rules, confirm the applicable legal requirements and review the entire user journey before launch. That process helps protect the campaign budget, reduces the likelihood of account disruption and supports more credible communication with prospective users.

Crypto-specific expertise matters because a technically strong campaign can still fail if its product category, claims or audience do not fit the rules. Enlight Business treats compliance awareness as part of sound crypto marketing strategy: it is easier to identify a problem before a campaign goes live than to recover the budget, access and trust lost after it does.

Ultimately, compliance is not separate from performance. It defines which audiences a project can reach, which messages it can responsibly use and which acquisition channels it can sustain over time.
