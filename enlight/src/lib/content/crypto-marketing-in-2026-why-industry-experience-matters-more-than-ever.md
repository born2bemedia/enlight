## Crypto Marketing in 2026 Requires More Than General Marketing Skills

Promoting a crypto project in 2026 means operating in a market where marketing decisions are closely connected to regulatory status, geographical restrictions, platform policies and the specific mechanics of crypto products. A campaign that works for a conventional technology company may not be suitable for a crypto exchange, wallet provider or blockchain foundation.

The market's latest developments make this distinction particularly important. A September 2026 public-record study, [State of Crypto Marketing 2026](https://northpoint.fi/resources/writing/state-of-crypto-marketing-2026/report.pdf), examined 27 crypto companies and 17 crypto-focused agencies, documenting changes in European market access, advertising eligibility, marketing teams, agency services and public disclosure. Its findings point to an industry where specialist knowledge is not simply an advantage: it is necessary to make informed marketing decisions.

For example, the study identified 324 legal entities authorised to serve crypto customers in the European Economic Area (EEA). Of these, 124 could serve customers in only one state, while 132 had permission to operate in 29 or 30 states. These figures describe regulatory permission, not actual customer reach. Nevertheless, they show why geographical targeting in crypto cannot be based on audience size alone.

A marketing team needs to understand where a company can legally operate, which audiences it can address and what restrictions apply before deciding how to promote its services.

## Regulation Has Become Part of Crypto Marketing Strategy

Regulation is often treated as a legal or compliance responsibility separate from marketing. In the crypto sector, that separation is increasingly difficult to maintain.

The report documents how the end of the MiCA transition period affected customer acquisition in Europe. On 1 July 2026, Binance and HTX stopped accepting new EEA customers because they did not appear in the relevant ESMA authorisation register. Gemini had already withdrawn from the European market in February 2026, despite retaining a licence covering 29 countries.

These developments changed the competitive environment. Six licensed exchanges ran promotional offers targeting customers affected by the market exits. Their campaigns included deposit bonuses, transfer rewards, cashback and prize draws.

The campaigns also differed in their conditions. OKX offered an 8% USDC bonus distributed over 52 weeks, while Bitpanda combined a €25 BTC reward with cashback subject to a 30-day holding period. Bitvavo advertised an annualised bonus with a higher rate dependent on trading turnover.

These are not interchangeable offers. Their value, eligibility requirements, holding periods and conditions affect how customers understand them and how marketing teams should communicate them.

A generalist team may be able to write promotional copy, but it may not recognise the implications of a reward distributed over a year, a bonus conditional on trading volume or a campaign restricted to specific European markets.

Crypto marketing experience helps teams identify these distinctions before they become problems in campaign messaging, targeting or customer expectations.

## Advertising Access Depends on Understanding the Rules

Regulatory authorisation can also affect which advertising channels a crypto company can use.

The study documents Google's advertising policy for cryptocurrency exchanges and software wallets. Since April 2025, advertising these services in the EU has required MiCA authorisation and Google's own certification. In August 2026, the policy was extended across the EEA and included hardware wallets.

This creates a direct connection between regulatory status and media planning. A marketing team cannot simply select Google Search, YouTube or Display because those channels perform well for another client. It must first establish whether the advertised service meets the platform's eligibility requirements.

This is particularly important for businesses operating across multiple markets or offering several types of crypto products. An exchange, a software wallet, a hardware wallet and a blockchain infrastructure provider may face different requirements. The advertising strategy must reflect the actual product and its regulatory position.

Specialist experience also helps teams identify alternative ways to reach relevant audiences when a paid channel is unavailable or restricted. Search visibility, public relations, educational content, community building and partnerships may all play a role, but their suitability depends on the project's objectives and market conditions.

## Crypto Campaigns Require Product and Market Knowledge

The promotional activity documented in 2026 also illustrates why crypto campaigns require a deeper understanding of the products being marketed.

Six exchanges competed for customers affected by regulatory changes, but their offers used different reward structures, eligibility criteria and timeframes. Some required users to transfer assets from another platform. Others required a deposit, a purchase, opt-in or continued activity.

The differences matter because campaign performance cannot be evaluated by comparing headline percentages alone. An advertised rate may be conditional, annualised or distributed over an extended period. A prize draw cannot be compared directly with a guaranteed reward without knowing the number of eligible entries and the probability of winning.

The study also notes that none of the six exchanges published campaign results. This means the available public information establishes what companies offered, but not which campaign generated more customers or delivered a better return.

For marketers, the implication is clear: understanding the mechanics of a crypto product is essential to planning and evaluating its promotion. Teams need to know what the customer must do, what the offer actually provides and which actions represent meaningful conversions.

The same principle applies beyond exchanges. A DeFi protocol, wallet, tokenisation platform and blockchain infrastructure provider each has different user actions, value propositions and conversion paths. Treating them as interchangeable crypto products leads to generic messaging and weak performance measurement.

## The Agency Market Is Crowded, but Expertise Is Hard to Verify

The number of agencies presenting themselves as crypto marketing specialists does not automatically make it easier for projects to find the right partner.

The study examined 17 crypto-focused agencies and found that ten named at least one of the 27 companies in its research as a client. Seven of those ten companies appeared on the websites of two or more agencies. KuCoin was named by three agencies in the initial review.

However, none of the companies had confirmed the agency relationships documented in the study. An agency's client list is therefore not necessarily independent proof of a current or completed engagement.

The services offered by these agencies also vary considerably. Some focus on influencer marketing, community building and paid campaigns. Others emphasise blockchain public relations, search visibility, go-to-market strategy, investor outreach or AI-related services.

This variety reflects the complexity of crypto marketing, but it also makes agency evaluation more important. A long service list does not establish that an agency understands the specific challenges of a project.

For example, an agency experienced in influencer campaigns may not have the expertise to plan a regulated exchange's European acquisition strategy. A team specialising in blockchain PR may not be equipped to improve a DeFi protocol's product onboarding or measure the quality of wallet activity.

Projects should evaluate agencies based on relevant experience, the people responsible for delivery, the methods used to measure results and evidence of work in comparable market conditions. Logos and broad service descriptions are not enough.

## Marketing Teams Need to Understand Both Crypto and the Changing Digital Landscape

The report also documents significant changes in marketing talent and the adoption of AI across the industry.

Seven senior Coinbase marketers, including its former CMO, were identified as having joined OpenAI or Anthropic. Four of these moves occurred between September 2025 and August 2026. The study also recorded 26 public workforce contractions during its research period, nine of which were framed as AI-related by media coverage. Only five were attributed to AI in the companies' own statements.

These figures should not be interpreted as evidence that AI is replacing crypto marketing teams. They show that the industry's talent landscape is changing and that the relationship between marketing expertise and AI is becoming more complex.

AI tools can help teams produce content, analyse data, automate workflows and support campaign operations. However, they do not automatically provide an understanding of crypto market structures, regulatory requirements, product mechanics or the expectations of a specialised audience.

A team still needs people who can assess whether a message accurately describes a crypto product, whether a campaign is suitable for its target market and whether the results indicate genuine business value.

The most useful role of AI is to support informed marketing decisions, not replace the industry knowledge required to make them.

## Why Marketing Transparency Matters in Crypto

Another finding highlights a persistent problem in the sector: the lack of publicly available information about marketing operations.

Of the 27 companies examined, 18 had not publicly disclosed a marketing budget or marketing team size. Only three companies in the study filed marketing-related figures with the SEC. Coinbase reported $1.06 billion in sales and marketing expenses for 2025, equivalent to 15% of its net revenue.

The figures are not directly comparable across all companies because they use different definitions and reporting periods. Some cover sales and marketing together, while others refer specifically to advertising or partnerships. Still, the lack of consistent disclosure makes it difficult to establish industry-wide benchmarks.

For crypto projects, this creates an additional challenge when planning budgets and assessing external partners. Generic claims about typical marketing costs or expected returns may have little relevance to a specific business model, target market or growth stage.

A reliable strategy should therefore start with the project's own objectives and measurable performance indicators. Teams need to define what counts as a qualified lead, an activated user or a valuable customer before setting campaign targets. They should also distinguish between marketing activity, acquisition costs and actual business outcomes.

## What Crypto Projects Should Look for in a Marketing Partner

The developments documented in 2026 suggest that crypto companies should assess potential marketing partners against a more specific set of requirements than general digital marketing experience.

| What to evaluate | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Crypto industry experience | The partner should understand the differences between exchanges, wallets, DeFi protocols, blockchain infrastructure and other crypto businesses |
| Regulatory awareness | Campaigns must reflect the markets a project can serve and the restrictions affecting its advertising |
| Product knowledge | Messaging should explain the actual product, its conditions and its value without misleading claims |
| Channel expertise | The partner should know how to combine paid media, SEO, PR, community building, content and partnerships according to the project's needs |
| Measurement and reporting | Performance should be evaluated through relevant business outcomes, not only traffic, impressions or engagement |
| Verifiable experience | Case studies, references and clearly defined responsibilities should support claims of specialist expertise |

These criteria help separate genuine industry knowledge from a generic service offering with crypto terminology added to it.

A suitable partner should also be able to explain why a particular channel or campaign mechanic fits the project, which limitations apply and how the team will assess the results. This is more useful than a promise to deliver growth through an identical package of services for every client.

## The State of Crypto Marketing in 2026: Expertise Is a Business Requirement

Crypto marketing has become increasingly connected to regulation, product complexity, market access and the changing digital advertising environment. The ability to create content or manage campaigns remains important, but it is not enough to navigate these conditions successfully.

The 2026 market evidence shows that regulatory permissions can determine which customers a company may acquire, advertising policies can restrict access to major platforms, and campaign mechanics can substantially change what an offer means to users. At the same time, the agency market contains overlapping client claims, inconsistent public pricing and limited evidence of campaign outcomes.

These conditions make industry-specific experience essential when selecting channels, developing messaging, managing reputation and evaluating performance.

For crypto projects, the right marketing partner is not simply an agency that offers SEO, PR, paid advertising or community management. It is a team that understands how these activities work within the crypto sector and can connect them to the project's actual business objectives.
